Michigan driveways take a beating. Between freeze-thaw cycles, road salt, snow plows, and daily traffic, the material you choose matters more here than almost anywhere else in the country.

After 25 years of installing driveways across Oakland and Macomb Counties, we've seen what lasts and what doesn't. Here's our honest take on the best paver options for Michigan homeowners.

## Brick Pavers: The Gold Standard

Brick pavers remain the most popular choice for Michigan driveways, and for good reason:

- **Freeze-thaw resistant** — quality clay pavers absorb minimal water, so they don't crack when temperatures swing
- **Easy to repair** — if one paver cracks or stains, you replace that single unit instead of tearing out a whole section
- **Excellent traction** — the textured surface provides grip even when wet or icy
- **25+ year lifespan** with proper installation and base preparation

We work with **Unilock**, **Belgard**, and **Techo-Bloc** pavers, all of which are engineered for northern climates.

## Concrete Pavers: Versatile and Cost-Effective

Modern concrete pavers have come a long way. Today's options are denser, more durable, and come in a wider range of colors and textures than ever before.

**Best for:** Homeowners who want a clean, contemporary look at a moderate price point. Concrete pavers typically run **$20–$40 per square foot installed** in the Rochester Hills area.

**Watch out for:** Color fading over 10–15 years. Sealing every 3–5 years helps maintain the original look.

## Natural Stone: Premium and Timeless

Bluestone, granite, and travertine driveways make a statement. They're the most expensive option but deliver unmatched curb appeal.

**Cost:** $40–$70+ per square foot installed, depending on the stone type and pattern complexity.

**Michigan tip:** Avoid softer stones like sandstone for driveways. They don't hold up to salt and heavy vehicles. Stick with granite or dense bluestone.

## What About Stamped Concrete?

We get this question a lot. Stamped concrete looks great on day one, but in Michigan's climate it tends to:

- Crack at control joints within 5–8 years
- Lose its color and texture from salt exposure
- Require full-section replacement when damage occurs (you can't patch stamped concrete invisibly)

For the same budget, interlocking pavers will last longer and look better over time.

## The Most Important Factor: Base Preparation

No matter which paver you choose, the base is everything. In Michigan, we install a minimum **8-inch compacted gravel base** for driveways, with proper grading for drainage. A paver is only as good as what's underneath it.
