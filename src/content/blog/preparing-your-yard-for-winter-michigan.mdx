Michigan winters don't just test your patience — they test your landscape. Proper fall preparation is the difference between a yard that bounces back in spring and one that needs expensive repairs.

Here's what we recommend for homeowners in Oakland and Macomb Counties before the first hard freeze.

## Lawn Preparation

### Final Mow
Lower your mowing height to **2.5 inches** for the last cut of the season. This reduces snow mold risk — a common problem in Michigan where snow sits on tall grass for months.

### Fall Fertilizer
Apply a **winterizer fertilizer** (high potassium, like 10-0-20) in late October or early November. This strengthens root systems and helps grass survive dormancy. It's the single most impactful application of the year.

### Leaf Removal
Don't let leaves sit on your lawn over winter. A thick leaf mat:
- Smothers grass and causes dead patches
- Traps moisture that promotes disease
- Creates habitat for voles (a real problem in SE Michigan)

Mulch thin layers with your mower or rake and bag heavy accumulations.

## Protect Your Plants

### Wrap Evergreens
Burlap wraps or anti-desiccant spray protect arborvitae, boxwood, and holly from winter burn. This is especially important for plants near roads where salt spray is an issue.

### Mulch Beds
Apply 2–3 inches of mulch around perennials and shrubs. This insulates roots against freeze-thaw damage. Pull mulch a few inches away from tree trunks to prevent moisture rot.

### Cut Back Perennials (Most of Them)
Cut dead perennial stems to 4–6 inches. **Exceptions:** Leave ornamental grasses and late-winter interest plants (like sedum) standing until spring.

## Hardscape and Irrigation

### Blow Out Your Sprinkler System
This is non-negotiable in Michigan. Any water left in irrigation lines **will freeze and crack pipes**. Professional winterization uses compressed air to clear all zones.

We recommend scheduling this before mid-October — once temps consistently drop below 40°F, you're in the danger zone.

### Seal Your Pavers
Fall is the ideal time to apply paver sealer. The moderate temperatures allow proper curing, and the sealer protects against salt damage all winter.

### Check Drainage
Before the ground freezes, verify that your downspouts extend at least 4 feet from your foundation and that swales and drains are clear. Ice dams and frozen drainage channels cause flooding and foundation damage.

## Snow and Ice Planning

### Mark Obstacles
Place reflective stakes along driveway edges, garden borders, and any features that could be damaged by plows. This is especially important if you hire a snow removal service.

### Stock De-Icer
**Avoid rock salt on pavers and natural stone** — it causes spalling and surface damage. Use calcium chloride or magnesium chloride products instead. They're gentler on both hardscape and plants.
